BESTIARY

field notes of the last mages

Levy Footman

Pressed into the warlords' service for a copper a day. He raises his sword high — a white flash — then lunges in a straight line.

The lunge cannot turn. Step aside, jump it, or dash straight through.

Longbowman

Keeps his distance and drops arrows on you in long, lazy arcs. Backs away when you close in — but slower than you run.

The arc takes time. Move, and the arrow lands where you were.

Pavise Guard

He carries the Wall with him. Bolts splinter on his tower shield, and his shove has ended better mages than you.

The shield faces forward — be behind, above, or throw fire that falls. Frost shards punch clean through.

Sapper

A cheerful man with a bag of thunder. His bombs sputter where they land — then the ground remembers everyone standing near.

The fuse is mercy: move before it ends. His own masters aren't safe from the blast.

Wall Ballista

A siege engine bolted to the stone, crewed by no one you can see. Its arm flashes white before the bolt flies — flat, fast, and true.

It cannot fire through stone. Duck below its line, or dash the instant it flashes.

The Catapult Master

Quartermaster of the siege camp, wed to his machine. He keeps his distance and drops burning sky on you. The engine is timber and tar — it burns beautifully.

Watch where the boulder's shadow falls, and stand where it isn't. Fire turns his own machine against him.

Warhound

Bred for the hunt and starved for it. They run in twos and threes, low to the ground, and finish the last stretch in the air.

The lunge is committed the moment it leaves the ground. Dash through it — or greet it with a spell mid-flight.

Thornweaver

A druid who took the warlords' coin. He does not attack you; he attacks your choices — walls of living thorn, grown behind your heels.

Kill the gardener, then the garden. Brambles burn, and shatter twice as fast when chilled.

The Huntsmaster

Keeper of the packs, crowned in antler and bone. His crossbow is patient; his horn is not — every call brings more hounds.

Every third call leaves him breathless, horn glowing. That silence is yours to fill.

Old Thornheart

The forest's warden since before the Wall was a quarry. Roots that split the ground, dead wood dropped from the crown, and saplings that fight for him.

He is rooted — the arena is his reach, learn its edges. When the heartwood blazes, he takes double.

The Warden of Vhal

The Wall's last oath-keeper, armored in a hundred years of victories. He fights at spear's length: javelin volleys, a ground-splitting slam, and a charge like a falling gate.

Every assault leaves him winded — when the plume burns bright, he takes double. That is your window.

Gall-grub

The tree's larvae, fat with sap, hanging from the heartwood ceiling. They wait — and let go when you are under them. Where they burst, the floor is amber.

Look up. Two bolts will drop one before you are beneath it.

Rootling

A knot of living root that learned to walk. Slow, and it never hurries — because where it dies, a new root grows.

Kill it where you are not going. Frost makes the root it leaves brittle.

The Sapwright

A hunched thing that tends the tree's sap and throws it at the floor. Whatever it throws, it runs through untroubled — and you do not.

Answer a pool with your element before it charges: it will be thrown by your draft, slip on your ice, or seize on your charge — and stand winded. Strike then.

The Taproot

The Great Tree's true body, a root-mouth in black water. The bark turns spells. Only the open mouth is flesh — and when it withers, the floor goes with it.

Jump the surge, read the marks, and pour everything into the gape. After half, the edges sink: live in the middle.

The Horn of Bone

The warlords' last captain, on a beast the size of a horse, with the horn that bought a forest. It does not stand and fight. It runs the whole road, and everything you have killed runs with it.

Five grounds, one bar. Each ground has a rule that strips its armour; learn it, bleed a fifth, and chase. Dawdle and it heals. On the crown it drops the horn — pick it up.
